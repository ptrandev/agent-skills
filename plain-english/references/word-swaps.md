## Never cut

Each of these changes what the text claims. Carry them into the rewrite, in plain
words, every time. Deleting one is a fidelity failure, not a simplification.

- may, might, could, can (possibility, not fact)
- should, ought to (expectation, not fact)
- we believe, we expect, we think (the author's opinion, not a fact)
- according to, reportedly, said, per (attribution to a source)
- about, roughly, up to, at least, at most, more than, fewer than (bounds)
- some, most, many, few, all, none, only (quantifiers)
- if, unless, until, when, provided that, subject to (conditions)
- except, other than, excluding, apart from (exceptions)
- not, no, never, cannot (negation)
- currently, so far, as of <date>, in <year> (time limits on a claim)
- in <region>, for <group>, on <platform> (scope limits on a claim)
- estimated, projected, planned, targeted (not yet true)
- numbers, units, dates, currency, versions, names, percentages

## Context-dependent

Decide by meaning, not by the word. Cut only when the sentence claims the same
thing without it.

- essentially, effectively — hedge when it means "close but not exactly"; filler otherwise
- just, only — "only 3 users" is a bound; "just click here" is filler
- significant — a claim if the source gives the number, vague filler if it does not
- quite, rather, fairly — usually filler; a hedge in British-register understatement
- clearly, obviously — filler, unless the source gives the reason it is clear
- up to — always a bound, never filler ("up to 40%" is not "40%")
